Starboard Seating & Wine Decanting Strategy

When boarding at Pont de l'Alma for the 20:30 departure, request a table on the starboard (right) side of the glass salon. This positions your glasses directly facing the illuminated Musée d'Orsay, Louvre, and Notre-Dame on the outbound leg. On the standard Prestige tier (€130), your ticket includes a full bottle of AOC wine per couple (Mouton Cadet Blanc or Agneau Rouge Bordeaux) plus flutes of Champagne Castel Mouches with warm cheese gougères.

Cellar Secret: The Bordeaux reds are often stored on lower decks and poured at ~14°C. Ask your server to uncork the bottle the moment your starters are placed; giving it 20 minutes to reach room temperature (17°C–18°C) transforms the tannins before the Châteaubriand or roasted lamb arrives.

Avoid the 4-Person Wine Split: Why Privilège Service Pays for Itself

Pay close attention to drink terms: the entry-level Étoile (€115) and Découverte (€139) tickets allocate one bottle of wine for every four guests (or just half a bottle per couple) alongside basic white wine Kir. If you are celebrating an anniversary, upgrade directly to Privilège Service (€179): it guarantees lateral floor-to-ceiling window seating, provides a full bottle of selected AOC wine per couple (Mâcon-Villages or Saint-Joseph), includes genuine Champagne apéritifs, and adds an affineur cheese course.

Tasting Advice: If choosing the pan-seared sea scallops starter, request the Mâcon-Villages served well-chilled (around 9°C–10°C) to complement the sweet leek fondue. For red wine drinkers, skip the duckling main (consistently tough) in favor of the pan-fried veal medallion.

Verdict? Second best to Bateaux Mouches. Here's why:

  • Price: Bateaux Mouches' most expensive 4-course ticket costs 155€, while Bateaux Parisiens' middle tier tickets cost 139€ and 179€, while the highest tier ticket is 215€.

  • Food: The food at Bateaux Mouches is better and more unique, but you get the same amount of courses. There are 3-course and 4-course options on both ships.

Verdict - Bateaux Mouches or Bateaux Parisiens?

Now, this may sound one-sided. Am I saying you shouldn't book a Bateux Parisiens cruise?

Hell no!

They offer excellent cruises in both the sightseeing and dinner categories. They have the most reviews, too.

But as good as it is, I strongly felt that Bateaux Mouches to be better. I may be the only person in the world who has been a guest on all 5 major dinner cruises in Paris, so that's my credibility. And I'm in the business of ranking cruises, after all.

Even by objective standards (price, value, staff, ship, food), it's easy to see that you simply get more and better quality at Bateaux Mouches.

But I understand that not everything is so black and white, and you may have your own preferences and criteria. You will find everything here to help you make your choice.

Overall, Bateaux Parisiens secures a solid second place on my list.

Are Paris Dinner Cruises a Lame Tourist Traps?

While dinner cruises are popular with tourists, I wouldn't call them a "trap", which means someone is overcharging you for a mediocre experience.

By definition, if you pay a reasonable price for a fabulous experience, you got you're money's worth. In this case, just choose Bateaux Mouches, and you'll be all right.

I think the concept of dinner cruises on the Seine is great.

But yes, some of them aren't worth it - there's a significant gap in execution. Just read my article and you'll see the clear differences between the top and last items on this list.

Note: All cruises offer photography service, which is overpriced in my opinion (20 euros).

What to Wear for a Paris Dinner Cruise?

For virtually all dinner cruises, smart casual clothing does the job.‍

You go as high up in the formal department as you'd like, but never the opposite - don't go too casual or they might turn you away at the entrance. Too casual means shorts, casual t-shirts, flip flops, athletic wear, pyjamas, bathrobes and overly worn or ripped jeans.

The cruise staff still expect a certain level of refinement that matches the elegant atmosphere they're trying to create on board.

What Time of Year is Best for a Dinner Cruise?

Paris dinner cruises operate year-round, but each season offers different advantages:

  • Spring (April-June): Comfortable temperatures and longer daylight hours let you see more landmarks in natural light.
  • Summer (July-August): Warmest weather for open-air viewing, though these are the most crowded months.
  • Fall (September-October): Beautiful autumn colors along the riverbanks with fewer tourists.
  • Winter (November-March): Special charm with holiday lights and decorations, particularly in December.

Summer offers the latest sunset times, which means you might see both daylight and nighttime views during your cruise. However, shoulder seasons (spring and fall) often provide the best balance of good weather and smaller crowds.

Is There A Photo Service On Board?

  • Every cruise offers a professional photography service to capture the evening. Pricy, but could be worth it for high-quality family or couple photos as souvenirs. You can always inspect both the digital and physical photos before deciding to buy.
  • The photo service is most affordable at 15€ per photo on Bateaux Mouches compared to other cruises, which charge 20-25€ per photo.

Birthday Celebrations and Anniversaries

  • Every cruise I have tried has been accommodating for birthday celebrations. These take place by the end of the cruise, with a birthday musical cue, clapping, and a piece(s) of cake/dessert with firework candles.
  • Marriage proposals are also possible on certain cruises, just ask the company if they offer them
